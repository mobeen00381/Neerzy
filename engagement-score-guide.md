# How to Improve Your Engagement & Activity Score

Engagement & Activity is weighted at 15% in [Neerzy's free GBP audit](https://www.neerzy.com/gmb-audit-tool). It's the category most directly about *ongoing* behavior rather than a one-time setup — which makes it the clearest test of whether a profile is actively maintained or was set up once and abandoned.

## 1. What This Score Measures

- **Whether your profile is claimed**
- **Google Posts frequency and recency**
- **Post types** — offers, events, updates
- **Q&A section activity**
- **Product/service listings**
- **Booking links and CTAs**

## 2. Why Google Cares

Completeness tells Google what your business *is*. Engagement & Activity tells Google whether it's still *operating*. A profile can be fully filled out and still go quiet for a year — this category exists specifically to catch that gap, which is why it rewards frequency and recency over one-time setup.

## 3. Common Reasons for a Low Score

- **No Google Posts published in months**, even on an otherwise complete profile.
- **The Q&A section sits empty**, left for a stranger to post the first question rather than being seeded by the business.
- **Reviews go unanswered** — response rate is checked here as well as within Reviews & Reputation, since it's a genuine activity signal in its own right.
- **No booking link or clear CTA connected**, even when the business uses scheduling software that supports it.

## 4. Real Examples

A plumbing profile that's fully complete but hasn't posted in eight months, has an empty Q&A section, and doesn't respond to reviews will typically score in the 10–30 range on this category — the lowest range of any of the five, since every sub-metric here depends on ongoing action. A profile posting weekly, with a seeded Q&A section and a near-100% review response rate, will typically score in the 85–100 range.

## 5. Priority Checklist

- [ ] Publish at least one Google Post per week
- [ ] Seed your Q&A section with the 3–5 questions customers ask most often
- [ ] Respond to every review within 48 hours
- [ ] Connect a booking link if you use scheduling software
- [ ] List individual products or services if you haven't already

## How This Fits Your Overall Audit Score

At 15% weight, Engagement & Activity moves your overall score less than Completeness or Reviews, but it's the category most likely to quietly decay if left unattended — a strong score here today can slip within a few months of inactivity even if nothing else on the profile changes.

## Common Questions

**How many Google Posts do I need per week to score well?**
One per week is the practical baseline for a strong score; businesses posting two to three times a week, tied to real completed jobs, tend to score highest.

**What should I put in my Q&A section if no customers have asked anything yet?**
Post the questions yourself, as the business — "Do you offer emergency service?" or "Do you serve [neighboring town]?" are common, realistic starting points that also double as useful information for future visitors.

**Does responding to reviews really count as "engagement," separate from the review itself?**
Yes — the review's existence and rating are scored under Reviews & Reputation, while your response behavior is tracked separately here as a distinct activity signal.

**Do I need a booking link if I take all my bookings by phone?**
It's not mandatory, but it's checked as a sub-signal — if you use any scheduling software, connecting it is a small, one-time task that adds to this score with no ongoing effort required.

## 6. Common Mistakes

- **Posting once in a burst, then stopping** — recency is scored on an ongoing basis, so a single flurry of activity fades from the score within weeks.
- **Copy-pasting the same post repeatedly** — post type diversity (offers, events, updates) is part of what's checked, not just raw frequency.

## 7. When to Re-Run the Audit

Because this category depends on an established pattern, not a single action, give it at least two to three weeks of consistent posting and Q&A activity before expecting a large jump — a single post published the day before an audit run will barely move this score on its own.
