<h2 id="hvac-local-search-landscape">The HVAC local search landscape</h2>

Homeowners searching for HVAC help fall into two very different groups, and it matters which one you're optimizing for.

**Business owners searching for marketing help** — "hvac seo" (2,900/mo), "hvac seo services" (2,400/mo), "hvac seo company" (1,600/mo), "seo for hvac companies" (1,600/mo), "seo for hvac contractors" (1,600/mo), "hvac seo agency" (1,300/mo). If you're reading a guide like this one, you're this searcher — and Semrush rates the core term "easy" to rank for (KD 29%), which is why this guide exists.

**Homeowners searching for service** — this is a much larger pool, but most of the highest-volume terms are either extremely competitive or not worth chasing directly:

- `hvac` alone: ~301,000/mo, but KD 61 — essentially unwinnable through content for a single local business.
- `hvac repair`: ~110,000/mo, KD 13 — lower difficulty than the volume suggests, but still dominated by national directories and big platforms at that scale.
- A large share of "hvac" searches are actually job-seekers (`hvac trade school`, `hvac technician salary`, `hvac apprentice jobs`) or branded and navigational (`ferguson hvac`, `hvacdirect.com`) — not customers.

The real opportunity for an individual HVAC business isn't out-ranking national brands for "hvac" — it's showing up in the Local Pack (the map plus three business listings) for your service area, and capturing the lower-competition, high-intent searches below.

<h2 id="can-content-alone-rank-you">Can content alone get you into the Local Pack?</h2>

Being honest here, the same way this applies to every trade: no. Google's Local Pack is driven primarily by your Google Business Profile — its completeness, category accuracy, reviews, and activity — combined with proximity to the searcher. A well-written page on your own website helps your broader visibility and supports your profile, but it will not out-rank a competitor with a stronger, more active, better-reviewed Google Business Profile, however good your writing is.

What actually moves the needle, in rough order of impact:

1. Profile completeness and correct categories
2. Review volume and recency
3. Posting activity and fresh photos
4. Proximity and service-area accuracy
5. On-site content (helps, but last on this list — not first)

<h2 id="gbp-optimization-for-hvac">Google Business Profile optimization for HVAC</h2>

A few things are specific to HVAC and worth getting right:

- **Categories.** Your primary category should match what you're actually best known for (e.g., "HVAC Contractor"), with accurate secondary categories added for the specific services you offer — AC repair, furnace repair, duct cleaning. Google increasingly separates search intent by sub-category, so an incomplete category list can quietly exclude you from searches you'd otherwise win.
- **Service area.** HVAC businesses commonly serve a wider radius than, say, a locksmith. Set your service area accurately — too narrow and you miss real customers; too broad and Google may trust your relevance signal less for any single area.
- **Attributes.** Flag emergency and 24/7 availability, licensing, and insurance if Google's attribute options support it in your category — these directly match some of the highest-intent, lowest-competition search terms below.

<h2 id="seasonal-and-emergency-search">Seasonal and emergency search — HVAC's real opportunity</h2>

| hvac emergency service | ~6,600 | 12 — low |
| hvac cleaning | ~8,100 | 7 — very low |
| hvac duct cleaning | ~6,600 | 14 — low |

Two practical takeaways:

1. **"Emergency" is doing real work in this data.** These terms are both high-volume and low-difficulty at the same time, which is unusual — it means demand is real but few local competitors have built content or profile signals specifically around it. If your Google Business Profile and posts consistently mention emergency and same-day availability, you're targeting exactly this gap.
2. **Post seasonally, deliberately.** Shift your Google Post cadence and photo focus ahead of each season: AC tune-ups and repair-readiness content in spring, ahead of summer demand; furnace and heating-system content in fall, ahead of winter demand. A profile that only ever talks about one season looks inactive the rest of the year — the opposite of the "stays active" signal that helps Local Pack ranking.

<h2 id="reviews-for-hvac">Reviews for HVAC</h2>

Reviews matter for every trade, but HVAC reviews carry a specific kind of persuasive weight: a review that mentions a fast response during a breakdown ("came out same day in a heat wave," "had our heat back on within hours") does more to convince an anxious, urgent searcher than a generic five-star rating does. Ask for a review right after emergency and same-day jobs specifically, not just after routine maintenance visits. For the full mechanics of review timing and requests, see the [Reviews Score Guide](/reviews-score-guide).

<h2 id="photos-that-matter-for-hvac">Photos that matter for HVAC</h2>

- Before/after equipment photos (old unit vs. new install)

- Technicians in uniform, and branded vehicles — reinforces legitimacy for a trade where in-home trust matters
- Duct cleaning and other less-visible work, since customers can't otherwise see what they paid for
