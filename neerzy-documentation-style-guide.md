# Neerzy Audit Documentation — Style Guide

One page. Follow this for every audit-category guide, present or future, so quality and trust stay consistent regardless of who's writing.

## Required Sections, In Order

1. **Purpose** — one sentence, in the opening paragraph: what question does this page answer, and which audit category does it belong to.
2. **What Neerzy Measures** — the exact sub-checks within this category, stated plainly. Pull directly from the audit's real scoring logic — never guess or round up.
3. **What Neerzy Does NOT Measure** — explicitly name what's out of scope for this category, even if it seems obvious. This is what prevents overclaiming and is non-negotiable.
4. **Why Google Cares** — the underlying ranking-signal logic, not just "the audit checks this." Connects the audit back to real SEO value.
5. **Common Reasons for a Low Score** — realistic, specific patterns, not generic filler.
6. **Real Examples** — illustrative before/after scenarios. Realistic ranges are fine ("will typically score in the 20-40 range"); specific unverified statistics are not. Never state a claim as fact unless it reflects confirmed product behavior.
7. **Priority Checklist** — a short, actionable, checkbox-style list.
8. **How This Fits the Overall Score** — one paragraph connecting this category's weight to the bigger picture.
9. **Common Questions** — 4–6 real questions a user would actually search or ask, answered in 1–3 sentences each.
10. **Common Mistakes** — what NOT to do, especially anything that risks a Google policy violation.
11. **When to Re-Run the Audit** — honest timing expectations, calibrated to whether the category depends on customer behavior (slow) or direct field edits (fast).
12. **Related Guides** — links to the pillar, Understanding Your Audit Score, the bridge page, and any sibling category guides.
13. **What Improvement Should You Expect?** — a simple action → likely-impact table. Never assign a specific point value. "Quick / Medium / Long-term" or "Immediate / Within days / Within weeks" — not "+12 points."
14. **CTA** — always "run or re-run your free audit," linked to the real audit URL.

## Hard Rules

- **Never invent a statistic, threshold, or percentage that isn't confirmed by the actual product.** If a number in a draft isn't verified, mark it inline as `[NEEDS PRODUCT VERIFICATION]` rather than publishing it as fact, and route it to the product team before it ships.
- **Never assign a specific score-point increase to any action.** Directional impact only (quick/medium/long-term).
- **Never describe a maturity level, tier, or benchmark range without real usage data behind it.** "Illustrative" framing doesn't fix this — if it looks like a fact, a reader will treat it like one.
- **Every page must state what the audit does NOT check**, not just what it does. This is the single most important trust-preserving habit in the whole system.
- **Any claim about Google's own algorithm** (not the Neerzy audit) should be framed as established local-SEO practice, not asserted as confirmed internal Google logic — Google doesn't publish exact ranking weights, and guides shouldn't imply otherwise.

## Voice

Plain, direct, second person. Explain the "why," not just the "what." No filler adjectives ("amazing," "powerful," "game-changing"). A busy plumber with zero SEO background should be able to read any section and know exactly what to do next.

## Before Publishing Any New Guide

- [ ] Every audit-behavior claim fact-checked against the actual product by someone on the Neerzy team
- [ ] No invented statistics, thresholds, or point values anywhere in the page
- [ ] "What Neerzy does NOT measure" section present and accurate
- [ ] Linked correctly into the pillar, the bridge page, and Understanding Your Audit Score
- [ ] CTA links to the live audit URL, not a placeholder
