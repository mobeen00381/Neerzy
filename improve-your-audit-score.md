# How to Improve Your Overall Audit Score

You've run [Neerzy's free GBP audit](https://www.neerzy.com/gmb-audit-tool) and you have a number. Now the real question: **what do you actually do next?**

This page is the bridge between your score and a specific plan. It won't repeat what each category measures — that's covered in [Understanding Your Audit Score](/understanding-your-gbp-audit-score) and the individual category guides — this page is purely about sequencing: what to fix first, in what order, and what to realistically expect.

## Step 1: Find Your Lowest-Scoring Category

Your audit results break down into five categories, each with its own score out of 100 and its own weight in your overall total:

| Category | Weight |
|---|---|
| Completeness | 25% |
| Reviews & Reputation | 25% |
| Visual Content | 20% |
| Engagement & Activity | 15% |
| Local SEO Optimization | 15% |

Your overall score is the weighted average of these five. The category dragging your score down the most is usually your lowest-scoring category *among the two 25%-weighted ones* — Completeness or Reviews & Reputation — simply because they carry the most mathematical weight. A 30 in a 15%-weighted category hurts less than a 30 in a 25%-weighted one.

## Step 2: Fix in the Right Order

Not all fixes take the same amount of time or effort. Here's the realistic order, based on how quickly each category can move:

**1. Completeness first, if it's low.** This is entirely within your direct control — no customer behavior required — and most gaps can be fixed in under 20 minutes. See the [Completeness Score Guide](/completeness-score-guide).

**2. Visual Content second.** Uploading a batch of real job photos takes an afternoon, not weeks, and shows up on your very next audit run.

**3. Engagement & Activity third.** Publishing your first Google Post and seeding your Q&A section can be done same-day, though the *pattern* of weekly activity that fully satisfies this category builds over the following month.

**4. Local SEO Optimization fourth.** A NAP consistency check across directories and confirming your service areas takes an hour or two of focused work.

**5. Reviews & Reputation — start immediately, but expect it to be the slowest to show full movement.** Unlike the other four, this category depends on real customers acting, not just you updating a field. Start the request habit in Step 1 alongside everything else, but don't expect it to catch up to your other fixes overnight. See the [Reviews Score Guide](/reviews-score-guide).

The practical takeaway: **start reviews on day one, since it's the slowest-moving category, while knocking out Completeness, Visual Content, Engagement, and Local SEO in the same week.**

## Step 3: A Realistic Timeline

| Timeframe | What Typically Moves |
|---|---|
| Same day | Completeness fixes, seeding Q&A |
| Within a week | Visual Content (photo upload), first Google Post published |
| 2–4 weeks | Local SEO Optimization (NAP checks, service areas confirmed), Engagement pattern establishing |
| 4–8 weeks | Reviews & Reputation showing meaningful movement |

This isn't a guarantee of specific point increases — your starting score and local market both affect the pace — but it reflects the general order in which categories tend to respond.

## Step 4: Re-Run the Audit

Run the audit again after your first week of fixes to confirm the fast-moving categories (Completeness, Visual Content) have registered, then again at the one-month mark to see Reviews and Engagement catch up. Treat this as a recurring monthly habit rather than a one-time check — profiles drift back toward incomplete and inactive without ongoing maintenance, which is exactly what the [Neerzy WhatsApp workflow](/seo-for-plumbers#section-19) is built to prevent going forward.
