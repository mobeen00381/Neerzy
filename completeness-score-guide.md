# How to Improve Your Completeness Score

Completeness is one of two categories weighted at 25% in [Neerzy's free GBP audit](https://www.neerzy.com/gmb-audit-tool) — tied with Reviews & Reputation for the most influence on your overall score. The difference is that Completeness is almost entirely within your direct control and can be fixed in a single sitting, which makes it the best place to start if you're looking for the fastest visible improvement after your first audit.

## 1. What This Score Measures

The audit checks whether these fields are actually filled in on your Google Business Profile:

- Business name present
- Address present
- Phone number present
- Website link
- Business hours set
- Primary category set
- Business description written (up to ~750 characters)
- Attributes and services offered

Each of these is checked as present or missing — there's no partial credit for a vague or half-filled field, which is why profiles that look "mostly done" from a glance often score lower than expected here.

## 2. Why Google Cares

Google's local ranking algorithm relies on your profile to determine relevance — whether your business is actually a match for a given search. A missing category, an empty description, or unset business hours doesn't just look unfinished to a human visitor; it removes a direct signal Google uses to decide whether to show your business at all. An incomplete profile isn't a smaller version of a complete one in Google's eyes — it's a weaker relevance match, full stop.

## 3. Common Reasons for a Low Score

- **The profile was claimed years ago and never revisited.** Many plumbing businesses set up their listing once, filled in the bare minimum, and never returned.
- **The business description field was left blank**, or contains just a single generic sentence far short of the full character allowance.
- **Only a primary category is set**, with no secondary categories added even though the business genuinely offers multiple service types.
- **Attributes were never touched** — free estimates, licensed, veteran-owned, and similar tags exist but were never checked because the owner didn't know the section existed.
- **Business hours are set but not maintained** — accurate when created, now outdated after a schedule change.

## 4. Real Examples

A plumbing company with a name, address, and phone number filled in, but no business description, no secondary categories, and no listed attributes, will typically score in the 40–60 range on this category alone — a large gap for something that takes under 20 minutes to fix.

A business that has gone further — full description, multiple accurate categories, every applicable attribute checked, and current hours including holiday schedules — will typically score in the 85–100 range, with the only remaining gaps being genuinely minor.

## 5. Priority Checklist

Work through these in order — each is a direct field in Google Business Profile Manager:

- [ ] Confirm name, address, and phone number are accurate and exactly match your website
- [ ] Set your primary category to "Plumber" (not a generic "Contractor")
- [ ] Add every accurate secondary category (e.g., "Drainage service," "Water heater repair service")
- [ ] Write the full business description using close to the full ~750-character allowance
- [ ] List every individual service you offer, not just a general summary
- [ ] Check every applicable attribute
- [ ] Confirm business hours are current, including upcoming holiday hours

## How This Fits Your Overall Audit Score

Completeness is one of two categories weighted at 25% — the other being Reviews & Reputation. Together, these two categories make up half of your overall score, which is why a low overall score is often traceable to a gap in one or both of them rather than the three lower-weighted categories. Fixing Completeness fully, on its own, can move your overall score meaningfully even before touching anything else.

## Common Questions

**Can I leave the description blank if I don't know what to write?**
You can, but it counts as an incomplete field — even a short, factual paragraph naming your services and city scores better than leaving it empty.

**Does Google require a business description?**
No, it's optional at the platform level, but the audit treats a filled-in description as part of a complete profile, since it's a real relevance signal Google's own algorithm uses.

**Can I add multiple categories?**
Yes — one primary category and multiple accurate secondary categories. Adding categories that don't genuinely describe your services can violate Google's guidelines, so only add ones that are true.

**What if I work from home and don't want my address public?**
Google allows service-area businesses to hide their exact address while still listing service areas — this doesn't count against your Completeness score as long as the service-area fields themselves are filled in.

**How many services should I list?**
List every service you actually offer, individually, rather than one general summary — each specific service listed is a separate relevance signal.

**Can I change my categories later without losing progress?**
Yes, categories can be edited anytime with no penalty for changing them, as long as the updated categories remain accurate.

## 6. Common Mistakes

- **Keyword-stuffing the business name** (adding "24/7 Emergency Plumber" to a name that isn't your legal business name) — this violates Google's guidelines and risks suspension, which costs far more than the small relevance gain.
- **Choosing an overly broad category** ("Contractor" instead of "Plumber") to try to appear in more searches — this typically dilutes relevance rather than expanding it.
- **Writing a description full of generic marketing language** ("best plumber in town!") instead of specific, factual detail about services and service area — specificity is what actually supports relevance matching.
